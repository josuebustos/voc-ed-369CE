Step 4 - Reducing the Data 

Overview

For our research this semester, we want to narrow our focus to K-type main-sequence stars within the Milky Way galaxy.

Stars spend the majority of their lives on the main sequence, where they fuse hydrogen into helium in their cores. Once their hydrogen supply begins to run out, stars leave the main sequence and enter an evolved state, during which their outer layers expand. For reference, our Sun is currently a main-sequence star. Consequently, we can distinguish main-sequence stars from evolved stars using surface gravity measurements (logg in the dataset), since surface gravity weakens with radius according to g = GM/R2.

Finally, we only want to consider stars located within the Milky Way galaxy (indicated by MIPS_obstype in the dataset).

FITS Files - The Basics
What is a FITS File?

Flexible Image Transport System (FITS) files is a commonly used file-type in astronomy because it contains both your typical image data (pixel values at different pixel locations) and header information (such as instrument name, date of observation, and exposure time).

To access all this information, you can use specially designed software, such as DS9, or you can write your own Python script and handle these files using the modules built into Astropy. Using Python, you are also able to adjust the color stretch, color map, measure photometry, and even automatically locate objects, for example. The applications are multifold, but, here, we are interested in inspecting images for signs of contamination from nearby objects and various sources of contamination, ranging from cosmic rays to background galaxies. For examples of contaminated and clean images, see Figures 1 and 3 respectively from Identifying and Characterizing Infrared Excesses in the Spitzer Kepler Survey (SpiKeS)Links to an external site..

For more information about FITS files, read the article: A Primer on the FITS Data FormatLinks to an external site.

 
Where to access FITS files:

Since we are focused on photometric data from the Spitzer Space Telescope, we will retrieve FITS files from the Spitzer Heritage ArchiveLinks to an external site.. There will be a more thorough tutorial in the Astrophysics Research module.
